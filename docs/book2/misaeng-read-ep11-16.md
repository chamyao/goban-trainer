Source: the Toomics English translation (chapter numbers match Kakao's episodes: Ch. 11 is "Eleventh Move"), read
2026-10-10. Notes are paraphrase; quotes are short and at most three per episode. Toomics spells 고 과장 "Ko"; our cast id
and lines keep "Go".

**Big picture.** The fan sources had the right skeleton and wrong details almost everywhere:
(a) **Ep 11** is two phone calls with Han (at a small factory; the comic doesn't name the place) and Oh scolding Jang's
report layout. Oh's "all over the place" is real, but it is about **text boxes**, not Jang's focus. Jang offers to redo
the items; Han picks the second; Jang takes the presentation's design and asks "how old are you, punk?".
(b) **"My heroes are fading"** is a **dream in ep 12**, near its start, not a closing memory of newspaper clippings.
(c) **Ep 13**: the waybill sticks to Seok-ho's report **at Jang's desk**, because Jang put off the shredding; Seok-ho
asks the **lobby security desk** to bin it; the director kicks the paper box in silence; the interns are scolded on the
**roof**. Oh finds a torn scrap of Seok-ho's weekly report **glued to the waybill** at his own desk. **No one searches
the lobby bins.**
(d) **Ep 14**: Go's bragging (the supply-cabinet key on his key ring) confirms it to Oh. The confrontation is a chance
meeting in the street after drinks: "Get your intern his own glue!" Oh keeps it "a secret" to protect both interns.
(e) **Eps 15–16** are one day narrated by **Jang**: the 13th-floor clash is about a thick report (the dog meat is the
cliffhanger); the apology is **Jang's** idea (don't leave dead stones on the board), Oh acts on it, and **Kim Bu-ryeon
gets to Steve first**. The sauna is a dry sauna, no song. Episode 16 ends on an ordinary "to be continued".

**Episode 11 is also read, from the Korean original, in `misaeng-read-ep11.md` (the earlier Plot); m19–m19b follow that reading.**

**Corrections to the reader's notes below:** in ep 11 the red-eyed senior who scolds the layout **is Oh** (checked on the
page). The ep 13 and ep 14 notes disagree on the scrap: the page (ep 13's last panel) shows it glued to the waybill;

## 11수

**1. Scenes in order**
- *Title card.* "Eleventh Move". The frame-game diagram (1st Eung-ssi Cup final, Nie Weiping as White v Cho Hun-hyun as Black) shows Black 11 in the lower-left. It sits directly on top of an earlier black stone on the third line and joins it, pressing against the two white stones beside it. Nothing in the episode's text names the move.
- *A small factory yard (Han on location).* Han is on a site visit with an older factory man in a work sweater, handling a fabric sample. The man explains why his side should get most of the royalty on a specialty fibre. He tells Han to stay away from the technical detail and leave it to the experts. Han argues that someone selling the fabric abroad ought to understand it. The man answers that his job is to get the original sample and papers to the buyer on time, and that buyers either know the tech already or buy on liking. Han admits he speaks badly when he isn't confident and asks for help. The man softens and demands that Han buy him soju after work, and Han eagerly agrees. (Ep 10's site boss was harsh; this is a separate, warmer contact.)
- *Phone call 1 (Jang at his office desk; Han outside the factory, smoking).* Jang tells him he has emailed three more item proposals and asks Han to call once he's read them. Han sneers: is this an after-school hobby? As an intern at his old company, Han spent three or four days on a single item and wrote twenty pages for each one. Does Jang think this is easy? Jang calmly asks him to read them and offers to redo them if anything falls short.
- *Night, a lounge with leather sofas (Han alone at a laptop).* Han opens Jang's files and finds them 42, 40 and 53 pages long. He grumbles to himself that length proves nothing and that a proposal isn't something you dash off from a diary. Then he reads on, scrolling, and his face goes still. The drawing implies the work is good.
- *Next day, the Sales 3 office (Jang present, Han absent).* A red-eyed, spiky-haired senior (the one who later explodes in ep 12) tears into Jang over a report layout: clashing colours, text boxes everywhere, a mess. Jang should have followed the earlier finished documents; what did he do yesterday? Jang apologises. Kim (Assistant Manager) steps in and says Jang was busy with the intern PT test. The senior turns on Kim and asks whether he's at work to play. Kim apologises and has Jang agree to a quick redo. Kim then quietly coaches Jang: answering too fast only gets you shouted at longer, because you cut short the senior's time to yell. Jang drags text boxes smaller on command and yawns, short of sleep.
- *Phone call 2 (Jang steps into an empty meeting room; Han again outside the factory).* Han complains that interns are company employees too and asks whether Jang spent work hours on this. Jang ignores that and asks what Han thought. Han hedges. Jang offers: should he do it again? Han says he likes the second item. Jang asks whether he's sure, and Han says yes, decided. Jang then closes the deal his way. They chose the item together, and as the more experienced one Han loses nothing by teaming with him. Jang will build the details of the presentation to suit himself. He will share the work but won't take orders, as Han promised. Han splutters. Jang adds a final cheeky question about Han's age, calling him "punk" and mirroring what Han said to him earlier. Han, stunned, hiccups on the street; a "GASAN" pole sign is visible behind him.
- *Closing narration (city streets, then a hall full of baduk tables, then a photoreal pro's face).* Jang reflects. Baduk is at root a fight; you give way when cornered and sometimes live together. But it is also a world with a clear winner and loser, and he lived in it for over ten years. He's a straggler now, yet he was trained to win, and he is a player who doesn't hand his opponent the first move (sente). Back in the meeting room, Jang on the phone: "So?" Han mutters "this punk…" To be continued.
