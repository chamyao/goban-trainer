# Book 2 research brief: Chinese RPGs, and what they mean for *Hulao Pass*

Ground rules (from you):
1. **Tell the novel faithfully.** Its events, its order, and its own words where it has them.
2. **One path, Pokémon-style.** Route, then town, then a big beat, then a roadblock opens. No side threads or branches.
3. **No forced link between story and puzzle.** The go problem sits at the scene's decision point. Its tactic doesn't have to mirror the story.
4. **About Book 1's length:** roughly 24 beats.
5. **Protagonists hand off** wherever the novel itself moves its focus.

## Part 1: What the games actually do

### 仙剑奇侠传 (Chinese Paladin, 1995): the template for a linear Chinese RPG

Nine chapters. Each runs on the same loop:

> **town hub → a small conflict in town → a dungeon → a boss → a cutscene that adds or loses a companion and points to the next city**

| Ch. | Places | What gates it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 仙灵岛 | 余杭 inn → 仙灵岛 → 十里坡 → inn | fetch (the aunt's medicine, a hammer to cross); statue-order puzzle; boss (Miao warriors) |
| 2 比武招亲 | 苏州 → 林家堡 → 隐龙窟 | duel; a tournament; a rescue dungeon |
| 3 白河寒秋 | 白河村 → 玉佛寺 → three mazes → 鬼阴山 | herb fetch list; bosses; **scripted loss** |
| 4 扬州擒贼 | 扬州 → 蛤蟆山 → mountain inn | detective case with a two-day deadline; secret lever |
| 5 京城蝶舞 | the capital → 黑蜘蛛林 | 99 jugs of wine; "survive 7 turns" fights |
| 6 蜀山妖塔 | 蜀山 → 9-floor tower | talk to the right ghost; seven bosses in a row; 月如 dies |
| 7–9 | 灵山 → 大理 → 南诏 | fetch; a time loop; 灵儿 sacrifices herself |

Lessons:
- **The party changes about once a chapter**, and that change *is* the chapter's ending. This matches chapters 3–9 of our novel, which keep handing the story to someone new.
- **Each tragedy is rehearsed first.** 灵儿's mother makes the same sacrifice one chapter before 灵儿 does. A side romance (彩依 and 刘晋元) plays the main romance in miniature.
- **Gates come in many kinds:** fetch list, talking to the right person, scripted loss, endurance fight, deadline. This keeps a long linear path from feeling like the same beat over and over.
- **Townsfolk have three jobs:** they foreshadow ("fairies live on 仙灵岛"), add texture (the beggar's monologue), and mirror the theme.

*Sources: gamegene.cn/wiki/358–366 (9-chapter walkthrough); zh/en Wikipedia 仙剑奇侠传.*

### 仙剑奇侠传三 (2003): how to switch viewpoint

- Most of the game follows 景天. Short cutaways go to other threads, such as 雪见 fleeing 唐家堡 or 重楼 pawning a sword.
- **Every cutaway ends in a meeting.** 雪见 literally collides with 景天. The handoff happens where the two threads touch.
- A companion "leaves for 蜀山" and comes back with news. Leaving the party stands in for an offscreen thread.

### 轩辕剑外传 汉之云 (2007): a story that stays inside recorded history

It's set in 231–234 (Zhuge Liang's last campaigns), so it doesn't cover our chapters. What it teaches is method:
- The heroes **witness or bring about** recorded events, but only on a small scale.
- **Every attempt to change a recorded outcome fails** for a reason inside the story (rain saves Sima Yi).
- **Known tragedies happen exactly as written.** Comfort comes only from the personal story.

Our version is simpler and stricter. We invent no heroes or causes; the novel's own cast acts.

*Sources: zh.wikipedia 軒轅劍外傳 漢之雲; gamersky.com/handbook/200801/94267.shtml.*

The pattern is clear: **every game has Hua Xiong and Hulao, and almost none tells the rest.** The Red Hare bribe, the Lü Boshe killings, the chain plot and Wang Yun's fall are mostly skipped, rewritten or turned into what-ifs. *Koei's 曹操传 even turns the novel's events into dialogue choices.*

**This is our opening.** A faithful version of chapters 3–9 would be unusual. The skipped scenes are also the strongest drama in the arc, and dialogue-driven scenes suit our engine better than battles do.

### 金庸群侠传, 侠客风云传, 古剑奇谭, 黑神话：悟空

Mostly **ruled out**. 金庸群侠传 is an open hub with stories you can take in any order. 侠客风云传 fires events by calendar and has 17 endings. Several gate on morality scores. All of that is branching.

**Kept:**
- **章回体 framing.** *Black Myth* numbers its chapters as 回 with paired titles, the way classical novels do. *Our novel already has a paired-line title for every chapter.* For chapter 5 it's 发矫诏诸镇应曹公 / 破关兵三英战吕布 ("A forged edict, and the lords answer Cao Cao; the pass army broken, three heroes fight Lü Bu"). Book 1 already ends on a scroll in the same storyteller voice ("Hear the next chapter").
- **Each act closes with a painted, reflective coda.** *Black Myth* ends each chapter with a short film that comments on the chapter instead of moving the plot on. The novel already does this with its "a later poet wrote" verses. Those verses become still-and-poem closers.
- **Each act tells one complete story about one group** (*Black Myth*, *GuJian*).
- **Town → challenge → reveal** (*GuJian*, *Chinese Paladin*).

*Sources: wiki.biligame.com/wukong/章回; gcores.com/articles/187061; gamersky.com/handbook/201007/162084.shtml; en/zh.wikipedia Heroes of Jin Yong, 俠客風雲傳.*

### Pokémon (the structure you asked for)

- **Route → town → gym → roadblock.** The next route opens only after the town's main event.
- **Roadblocks are diegetic.** Something in the story is in the way: a guard, a sleeping Snorlax, a flooded route.
- **A recurring rival.** Ours must be someone the novel itself keeps returning to, which is Lü Bu (Ding Yuan's camp, Hulao, the chain plot, Dong Zhuo's death).

## Part 2: Rules for Book 2

1. **The novel's events, in its order, in its words.** I translate from the Chinese original (Wikisource) and check meaning against Brewitt-Taylor (1925, public domain). The English is my own plain wording.
2. **One path.** Every node is `main`; edges form one chain. Gates on that chain must be **obstacles the novel itself contains**:
   - Liu Bei can't sit in the coalition council until he comes as Gongsun Zan's man.
   - Sishui is held by Hua Xiong; Hulao by Lü Bu.
   - You can't reach Dong Zhuo except through Lü Bu.
3. **Follow whom the novel follows.** Each handoff happens at a meeting, as in *Chinese Paladin 3*: one protagonist ends the scene, and the next one is in the room.
4. **Pace like *Chinese Paladin*.** Each place has town folk, a small scene, a big beat, and then the party changes or the road opens.
5. **Rehearse before the payoff, using the novel's own echoes.**
   - Lü Bu kills adoptive father number one (Ding Yuan, ch. 3) for a horse and gold, then number two (Dong Zhuo, ch. 9) for a woman. The player sees the first, so they see the second coming.
   - Cao Cao's "I'd rather wrong the world" (ch. 4) is set up by Chen Gong freeing him an hour earlier.
6. **Show the scenes other games skip.** Red Hare, Lü Boshe, the chain plot and Wang Yun's fall get full scenes. The brutal moments happen offscreen or in a still, and narration says what happened.
7. **Every act ends on a novel verse and a still.** Act sections use the novel's chapter couplets on the storyteller scrolls.

## Part 6: Decisions so far (from the user)

- **Faithful to the novel** comes first; **one path**, no side threads.
- **Go problems: a mix.** Most sit on the *legwork* (persuasion, checkpoints, recruiting, gathering), shown before the scene as the way in. A few sit mid-scene, at the novel's real contests (duels, escapes, Diaochan's nerve). Settled scenes play with no board.
- **Tasks that fail in the novel: (b).** The player solves the problem and the story still fails as written. The problem is the attempt, and the novel decides the result.
- **Act II is handed around:** Cao Cao raises the army; the brothers take Sishui and Hulao (and Panhe); Sun Jian takes the Luoyang ruins, the seal and the breakout; Cao Cao takes the lone pursuit.
- **Playing the villain is allowed:** e.g. Li Su bribing Lü Bu with Red Hare (ch. 3), Li Ru's schemes, Jia Xu raising the Liangzhou army to march on Chang'an (ch. 9).
- **Act III:** Wang Yun carries the legwork (gold crown, banquets, persuading Lü Bu, the conspirators); Diaochan carries the mid-scene contests (Phoenix Pavilion, the false suicide).
- **Length:** decide later.
- **The novel's task list** (≈160 tasks, chapters 3–9, with quotes) is the source for every place's legwork.
- **No M-of-N problem sets.** Every encounter is one problem, solved flawlessly, with the 30-second wait after a wrong move (this already exists and is good for learning).
- **Hard scenes have several problems**, with dialogue between them, at the turns the novel gives (the ridge, the Phoenix Pavilion, the sword on the wall, the palace gate). This needs the engine.
- **Design for the best game.** Don't limit stills or ask for less because of what exists today; reuse is a bonus.
- **Maps** are designed by a separate Places session from these docs (`docs/book2/`).
