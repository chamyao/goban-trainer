# 미생 착수0 – 10수, read from the comic (Kakao Webtoon), against Book 1 (m1–m6)

Source: viewport captures of https://webtoon.kakao.com/viewer/미생/<id> (ids 15097 … 15526), read 2026-10-10.
Notes are my paraphrase. Quotes are short (a phrase or one sentence) and limited to about three per episode.
Balloon counts are approximate. "Captions" means narration boxes and "balloons" means speech or thought.
"% carried" means the share of the episode's captions and balloons whose exchange appears in some form in our scene (narration or speech).

**Big picture.** The fan-comment research put several things in the wrong episode, and m1–m6 inherited the errors. The worst:
(a) **Ep 3** is Oh Sang-sik's episode. He forgot an **11 a.m.** meeting with an overseas buyer, and the call is from his **부장**, not a buyer, at 11 p.m.
(b) **Ep 4** is the café with the buyer, where the snapback is the buyer's baduk quiz. Oh's FOB talk is a phone call to Kim about a Malaysian claim. No CIF, no L/C, no glue stick.
(c) **Ep 5** has no "three errands". "앵그리 버디" is a pun on Kim Dong-sik as Jang's angry *buddy* (the intern buddy system).
(d) **Ep 7**: Kim's warning is "beware whoever approaches you first, because they want a dud partner to shine against". It is not about flatterers or favours.
(e) **The PT** is announced by Ahn in ep 7. Partners are **chosen, not drawn**, and Han **picks** Jang (ep 8–9).
(f) **Ahn's first scene** is the interns' study at the end of ep 5, not a corridor walk-by. She is a major voice in eps 6–10, and our m1–m6 barely use her.

## 착수0 (episode 0, id 15097)

**1. Scenes in order**
- *Title page.* 未生 is glossed as "not yet alive". An empty board, and the Ing Cup game-5 caption.
- *Childhood (montage, all narration).*
  - An uncle's toy, a set of stones, fascinates the small boy. After he says "단수" correctly, the uncle takes him to a neighbourhood baduk class, and his mother happily pays.
  - He learns fast and holds his own in the uncle's and father's betting games. He starts winning their money back from a local shop owner.
  - On the class director's advice he moves to an amateur 6-dan's academy. His mother is against it at first, the uncle and father doubt him, and nobody stops a nine-year-old who lays out book problems alone at dawn.
  - "Genius" becomes a sweet hypnosis for the parents. They send him to a professional's dojang. Their support burns hotter because the father's company has just gone under.
- *Trainee years.* At 11 he enters the Korea Baduk Association as a 연구생 (trainee). Around then his parents start clipping Lee Chang-ho's and Lee Sedol's rankings and prize money from the papers.
- *The failure.* "Seven years passed." A stone drops: he fails to turn pro. Only now does he notice his father's wrinkles and his mother's dulled eyes.
- *Leaving the association.* The world "sheds its skin". He insists he hasn't changed: tomorrow he would still be here, losing to younger, higher-ranked kids. Only the world in his eyes has changed, from blue and green to grey.
- *The excuses monologue.* Not talent, not bad luck, not the half-point losses, not baduk squeezed between part-time jobs, not parents who couldn't give pocket money, not his father's death and his mother taking to her bed. All of those would hurt too much, so he settles on "I didn't try hard enough".
- *The ending.* He walks off dropping stones from his pocket a few at a time. Last panel: crying under his hood.

Nobody wants anything from anyone here. It is pure interior: he wants an explanation he can bear.

**2. Dialogue.** About 30 captions and 0 speech balloons. Everything is Jang's narration.
- 「입단에 실패했다.」: "I failed to turn pro."
- The closing self-verdict (the research notes already quote it): he was thrown away only because he didn't try hard enough.

**3. Against our game (m1)**
- **In m1:** the father's ruin and the family's bet on the son (compressed); the mother's clippings; the excuses monologue; the grey world; the stones dropped a few at a time.
- **Staging (flagged in the code, but note the details are invented):** the examiner, "Last round. Begin.", the trainee opponent and the half-point game itself. The comic never shows the deciding game. It only says the half-point losses kept happening.
- **Not in ep 0:** "He is eighteen. This is the last chance… after this, he is too old." The comic gives 11 + 7 years. The age-limit framing isn't stated.
- **Wrong:**
  - m1 places the sponsor ("There's a job at my company. Come in on Monday.") straight after the loss. In the comic that job comes **years later, after his GED**, and it is told in ep 2. The Monday line is invented.
  - "He packs his desk without a word" conflates two things. The packing up with the "Really quitting?" question is **packing up his baduk life, asked by two trainee friends** (ep 2), not leaving the job.
- **Missing, worth adding:**
  - The childhood ladder (uncle → neighbourhood class → betting games → academy → pro dojang), at least as two or three lines.
  - Only after failing does he see his parents' ageing faces.
  - "Tomorrow I'd still be here, losing to younger kids."

**4. Carried:** about 45% of captions. The monologue half is there; the childhood half is missing.
