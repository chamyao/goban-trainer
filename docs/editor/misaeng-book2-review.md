# Editor's review: Misaeng, Book 2, "Challenge" (first draft)

_Reviewed cold from claude/plot-alt2 e700cb82: `docs/book2/misaeng-book2-script.md` and the raw steps in
`tools/tk_story_w22.py`. Book 2's maps aren't built, so the staging notes are against the plan's offsets.
Numbers in brackets are principles in `storytelling-principles.md`; A/B/C are the audits in `editor-playbook.md` E8
(A: cause before payoff; B: a motive for every action; C: time and place marked and matched by the staging)._

---

## 1. Verdict

**The second half of this book is the best stretch of the series so far.** From b23 to the end there's a clear
spine (the PT), real stakes ("Last time they started with thirty. Two got in."), a visible countdown, and a chain of
setups that all pay off inside the book:
- polypropylene in b23 → Han's swatches in b31;
- "Can we sell them together?" → "I'll answer when I've seen what you're selling me" → "let's sell those together";
- Oh's "sweat your feet in here" → the slippers;
- Park's shipping costs going up with oil → Ahn's freight answer;
- Han's mother's text about his medicine → the choke.

The climax is a boss board that means exactly what the scene means: Jang has to answer Han. The book ends quietly
at a memorial, without fanfare. The black-nails flashback and Han stepping into the slippers are the two strongest
images in either book.

**The first half (b17–b22b) is two good short stories that don't belong to Jang.** The Park Jong-gi arc (the
salesman who's too kind) has a complete shape: wound, push, crisis, confession and release. The daycare morning
(b22b) is the most moving beat in the book. But for six beats:
- the player mostly controls Park or Sun;
- Jang is a bystander or a meddler;
- the PT countdown that ended Book 1 ("Two weeks to the PT") disappears;
- every beat introduces new people.

A stranger who has just finished Book 1, wanting to know whether Jang survives the PT, meets a man they don't know
being sick on a strip of grass. This is the playtester's "events thrown at me" risk again (5, 10, 12). The opening
card even says so: "Until then, there's other people's work to watch."

**The fix is threading, not cutting.** The two stories already touch the spine in places (Ahn's freight answer;
"everyone plays their own baduk"; "He stands up" in both b20 and b29). Make those links visible, keep the clock
running, and give Jang a reason to be watching that serves the PT. Do that, and the first half reads as Jang's
education rather than a detour.

**Is it clear?** Mostly. The main confusions are: who "Jang Baek-gi" is; whose memory the black nails are; a few
lines with no prompt ("I hold grudges", "Don't call me 'punk'"); and a handful of staging gaps. **Gripping?**
From b25's 8pm text onward, yes. **Coherent?** It has two continuity breaks with Book 1 (the PT's venue, and Park's
years of service). **Shape?** Yes, once the first half is tied to the spine.

## 3. Beat-by-beat notes

**Opening card.** It bridges well from Book 1 and sets the stakes. "Until then, there's other people's work to
watch" tells the player the next six beats are a detour (5).
- **Fix:** give Jang a PT reason to watch, e.g. "Two weeks to the PT, and Jang has never seen a salesman at work.
  He's about to see two."

**b17 Bread on the Street.** Vivid and specific: the bun and milk, the stall, the alley. But it's all narration
about a stranger, with no board and no Jang (7, 8).
- "The time he reported the restaurant for giving the team free meat. Why is he always on the other side?" is
  unclear. Other side of what? The free meat example reads as scrupulous rather than soft.
- "He's happy. That's the trouble. He doesn't have the nerve to carry this much happiness" needs one clause of why
  (fear of losing it?).

**b17b An Exemplary Salesman.** Good: Jang inflates Oh's praise, and Park invents a victory and "feels lighter".
That's a real, slightly guilty piece of agency for Jang.
- The record quiz sits mid-scene between the flattery and the boast; Book 1's rule was quizzes open a beat (5, 19).
- Park reading the Ing Cup move as his own thought is odd. He doesn't play baduk.
- The resignation letter (promised in Book 1's closing card) goes into his jacket here and never comes out (4,
  audit A). **Fix:** at the end of b20, "He takes the envelope out of his jacket, and feeds it to the shredder." That
  also echoes Book 1's shredder.

**b18 Through the Door.** A good overheard reveal. The decision board poses the dilemma ("Keep the peace?") and the
win line holds the choice back (22). The wings still needs to read clearly on its own, or the "wings" image is
opaque.

**b19 The Proper Move.** Jang reads the president's anger as a trick: good use of his baduk lens. But his answer
(texting Kim to fake an urgent call, then announcing the president's promise out loud) looks like a counter-trick,
while the caption and narration call it "the proper move" and "answer it plainly" (14, 15).
- **Fix:** one line that names what's plain about it: "He takes the president at his word, out loud, where it can't
  be taken back."

**b20 Be Irresponsible.** The confession is the peak of the Park arc and it lands. "Every face around the table
becomes his own" is strong. "Everyone plays their own game of baduk", called back from Book 1, is a good cross-book
payoff.
- **Win line contradiction:** narration says "Park stands up", then the board's win line is "He stands up." He's
  already standing. **Fix:** win "He puts the note face down."
- **Continuity:** b17 says "twelve years", b18 and b19 say "ten years" (of the supplier relationship), and the
  director says "Four years?" If the director's mistake is the joke, show it: "Four years? — Twelve, sir." Otherwise
  it reads as an error.
- **Staging (C):** the narration lists a director, a department head and three teams, but only the director is
  spawned.

**b21 It's Personal.** Sun's introduction ("wiry, fast and exact") and her rules are efficient. The phone calls set
up the daycare cleanly.
- **Audit B:** "Ask Mr. Han. He's your partner. I hold grudges." answers a question nobody asked. What did Jang ask
  her, and what grudge? (See Q4.)
- **Sun's slide advice vs Kim's:** her "Fewer words, more pictures" contradicts Kim's "text on the slides is safer
  than pictures" in b25. That's potentially good, a choice Jang has to make, but nothing picks it up. Pay it off or
  drop one of them.

**b21b The Doorbell.** The doorbell image is excellent.
- **Caption:** "Keep them all alive" is the wrong verb for children waiting for their parents. **Fix:** "Every child
  at the door is hoping. Get them all home."
- **Stale comment:** the raw comment says "the record: Black 21", but the node is a go problem.
- **Unpaid setups:** Ms. Ha's "It feels like you like her" and Sun's "Thursday, lunch… come in at eight tomorrow".
  The 8 o'clock is nearly paid in b23 ("Sun is late this morning"). **Fix:** "Jang is in at eight, as told. Sun is
  late." The lunch is never paid (4, audit A).

**b22 The Drawing.** Strong and quiet. "He has golf tomorrow" says everything about the husband.
- **Staging (C):** the husband is washing up but isn't spawned or marked offstage.

**b22b Her Back.** The best beat in the first half. The decision board is exactly right (22), and "Not her face.
Her back" turns the drawing.
- **Spine:** Jang isn't in it. That's acceptable once, but the book now has five of its first six beats led by
  someone else (10).

**b23 Limited by Culture.** The spine returns ("Ten days to the PT… Two got in"). Han's polypropylene is a good
plant, and "From! The! Internet!" is a good joke against his field pose.
- **Audit A:** the first named appearance of "Jang Baek-gi" doesn't tell a stranger he's Book 1's glasses intern,
  and a second Jang next to "Jang:" (Geu-rae) is confusing (11). **Fix:** "Jang Baek-gi, the intern in glasses" on
  first mention. Consider "Baek-gi" in narration thereafter.
- **Staging (C):** Han "running across the plaza", but the beat is in the reference room.
- **Quiz placement:** the record quiz comes between the pitch and its punchline ("Is this what fieldwork… does to a
  person?"). Move it to open the beat.

**b24 The Question.** Han's "That's it. The intersection" is a good moment of the partnership working.
- **Unclear referent:** "Some of it they'll accept and some of it feels like a cage". Some of what? Name culture.
- **Unclear question:** "And we ask it of… the president" isn't understandable to a stranger. Ask it of whom, and
  why the president?
- **Han and baduk:** "Han plays fast baduk" reads as if Han plays. **Fix:** "Han thinks like a fast baduk player."

**b25 The Last Person.** The 8pm text is a great turn, and "the last person they'd want to sell to" is a clean
dramatic question.
- **Audit A:** "Don't call me 'punk', punk!" answers a "punk" Han never says on screen. Add his line.
- **Told, not shown (8):** the fight itself ("A fist, a grab, a scuffle") and the truce are narration. It's the
  partnership's low point, so give it at least one exchange.
- **Staging (C):** the copier and the advice happen in the office, but the beat is on the forecourt. **Fix:** "That
  evening, outside the tower…"
- **Quiz placement:** the quiz separates the HR question from its gloss. Move it.

**b26 Through Others.** "The other person will tell you who you are" is the book's best thematic line. The
deliberation board with the answer held back until b32 is excellent suspense (9, 22). The night find in Sales 3 makes
the discovery the player's own (20).
- **Audit A:** the find gives only the slippers ("Oh's slippers"), but in b32 Jang also sells "my boss's dress
  shoes". **Fix:** the find text includes both.
- **Wording:** b27's "Han sees Jang's slippers" reads as if Jang is wearing them. Is he? If so, say "Oh's slippers,
  on Jang's feet". It's a lovely image.

**b27 Next!** The parade of failures builds pressure well. "Because we're being interviewed for the job" is cold and
in character.
- **Name:** "The Jangku Team" sounds like Jang's team.
- **Staging:** Han isn't spawned, though he mutters beside Jang.
- **Weak ending:** the beat ends on a history quiz.

**b28 Nerves.** The medicine reveal is great dramatic irony: the player knows and Han doesn't (9). The hand-off
lets the player walk Han to the front, which is good play.
- **Optional plant:** in b25, Han's hands shaking with the stopwatch.

**b29 Black Nails.** The choke, the hand-off and the decision board are all right.
- **Audit C:** the black-nails memory isn't attributed. After the examiner asks *Jang* about the field, a stranger
  will take the boy and his father as Jang's memory until the next line. **Fix:** "Han hears Jang's 'No, sir', plain
  and unashamed, and remembers:" That also gives the memory its cause (A).
- **Raw staging:** after `["party", ["ms_jang"]]`, Han is no longer on the map, but he speaks again ("Well done, Mr.
  Jang"). Spawn him at the podium after the hand-off.
- **"His teacher":** say "his old baduk teacher" once. No teacher was introduced in Book 1.
- **Optional echo:** this "He stands up" echoes Park's. Making it explicit ("Like Park, he stands up") would tie the
  first half to the climax.

**b30 The President's Daughter?** Ahn's freight answer pays off Park's oil line. Oh's "his limit, for now. I'd like
him to stay" pays off later.
- **Whose thoughts:** "Would she ever do that for him?" Him: Lee, her partner? Name him.
- **Raw staging:** Han, who presents and counts in this beat, is never spawned.

**b31 The Voice of the Field.** Excellent. The decision board's opening thought states the paradox Sun warned of
(22), and "I want to sell them with Mr. Han" is the heart of the book.

**b32 Combat Boots (boss).** The climax fits the board (14). Book 1's dead-stone lesson returns, and the slippers on
Han's feet pay everything off.
- **Unclear line:** "You were amazing? I've done this a few times before." Who said "amazing"? Make it "Jang tells
  Ahn she was amazing. She shrugs: I've done this a few times before." (This plants b33b's reveal.)
- **Raw staging:** Ahn speaks but isn't spawned.
- **Audit C:** "the interns clear their desks into boxes" happens on the PT-hall map.
- **Missed payoff:** Oh is in the audience while his own shoes and slippers are sold. One reaction from Oh (looking
  at his feet) would pay off the find.

**b33 Contract.** Holding Jang's result behind a board, after the others' results, is good suspense.
- **Stakes unclear:** what "contract worker for two years" means isn't clear to a stranger. Is it a win, or less
  than the others got? **Fix:** "The others are permanent staff. He has two years." This also completes Book 1's
  "parachute" thread and gives Book 3 a stake.

**b33b The First Day.** "The team whose leader asked for him" pays off b30. The suit prank is warm and funny.

**b33c The First Morning.** A quiet, right ending (23).
- **Missed payoff:** the memorial is for workers fired after a strike, which is Han's father's story from b29, but
  Han has no beat there. **Fix:** "Han bows longest." That one line turns the memorial into the book's last payoff.
- **Optional motive (B):** a reason from Oh for bringing them, e.g. "Before you start, remember who doesn't get to."

## 4. Top ten problems, ranked

1. **The first half has no PT clock and no Jang-centred reason to be there** (b17–b22b).
   - **Fix:** a countdown line in each beat ("Thirteen days to the PT…").
   - Rewrite the card so Jang sets out to watch real salesmen for his PT.
   - End each episode on what Jang takes from it: Park → "you can't play another man's game"; Sun → what a back
     shows (5, 10, 12).
2. **Raw staging gaps where speakers aren't on the map:**
   - Han in b29 (after the hand-off) and in b30;
   - Ahn in b32;
   - the meeting room in b20;
   - the husband in b22;
   - Han in b27;
   - the room/narration mismatches in b23 (plaza vs reference room), b25 (copier vs forecourt) and b32 (desks vs
     PT hall). (C)
3. **The black-nails memory is unattributed** (b29). **Fix:** "Han hears Jang's 'No, sir'… and remembers:" (A, C)
4. **Lines with no prompt:** "Don't call me 'punk', punk!" (b25); "Ask Mr. Han… I hold grudges" (b21); "You were
   amazing?" (b32). (A, B)
5. **Unpaid setups:** the resignation letter (b17b; promised in Book 1's closing card); Thursday lunch (b21b); Sun's
   "fewer words, more pictures" against Kim's "text is safer"; Ms. Ha's interest, unless it's Book 3's. (4)
6. **Continuity with Book 1:**
   - Book 1 (m14) said the PT would be "a whole week at the training centre". Here it's two days in a PT hall at One
     International.
   - Park's years: twelve, ten and "four?". (13)
7. **Record quizzes mid-scene again** (b17b, b23, b25, b27), breaking the rule agreed in Book 1. b33's placement is
   good suspense; keep that one. (5, 19)
8. **The stakes of the result:** "contract worker for two years" needs its meaning (b33).
9. **Names:** Jang Baek-gi unintroduced as the glasses intern; two Jangs; "the Jangku Team"; ten-plus new named
   characters in the first half. (11)
10. **Small decision-board and caption fits:**
    - b20's win line "He stands up" (he's already standing);
    - b21b's "Keep them all alive";
    - b19's "proper move" that looks like a trick;
    - Park thinking a baduk thought in b17b. (14, 15)

- **Setup and payoff, b23–b33:** polypropylene → swatches; "sell them together" ×3; "sweat your feet in here" →
  slippers; oil and freight; the medicine text → the choke; "I've done this before" → Ahn's file; "I'd like him to
  stay" → "the team whose leader asked for him".
- **The boss board** as Jang's answer to Han. The slippers on Han's feet, then slippers under every desk.
- **The b26 deliberation board** holding the answer back until b32, and the night find as the player's discovery.
- **The b22b daycare door**, and the b22 drawing.
- **The 8pm text.** "The other person will tell you who you are."
- **The cold Ahn**, consistent with Book 1's revision.
- **Cross-book callbacks:** "everyone plays their own baduk"; dead stones; the shredder, if the letter fix is
  taken.
- **The quiet, fanfare-free ending.**

## 6. Questions for the writer

1. Is the first half meant to be Jang's education for the PT? If so, what does he take from Park, and from Sun?
2. Does Park resign, tear the letter up, or keep it?
3. Is "Four years?" the director misremembering, deliberately?
4. "I hold grudges": what is Ahn answering, and what's the grudge?
5. What does "we ask it of… the president" mean in Han's PT plan?
6. Is Jang wearing Oh's slippers on PT day? Did Oh know?
7. Book 1 said a week at the training centre; is Book 1's line or Book 2's staging the one to change?
8. Is Ms. Ha's interest a Book 3 thread?
9. Is "contract worker" a lesser hire than the others'? Will that drive Book 3?
